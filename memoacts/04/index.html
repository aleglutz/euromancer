1 — The cut as method

Part one · 7 min

  1. Montage is born where reality has stopped holding together on its own: urbanisation, the First World War, revolutions, Dada, collage. Not a device but a reaction.
  2. The Kuleshov effect: the meaning of shots changes in sequence.

2 — The cut as connection

Part one · 19 min · constructive, utopian montage

⏵ The Battle of Russia

(1943), dir. Frank Capra — film 5 of the Why We Fight series, U.S. War Department, 1942–45

27:26 — 30:52 youtu.be/WrKDBFJoo2w Player starts and stops on the marks · 3 min 26 s

Questions

  1. Where do you think each shot was filmed? How do you know?
  2. Mentally switch off the sound. What is left of the argument?

Critique according to Bazin. The shots are given the logical structure of oratory, and that speech acquires the credibility of a photograph. An illusion of proof arises where there are only ambiguous facts held together by the commentator's words. The invisible commentator, whom the viewer forgets about, is "the historian of tomorrow", resurrecting faces and events from the world's film archives at will. The objection is not to the content and not to the authors' being right, but to the method.

Bazin, "On Why We Fight: History, Documentation, and the Newsreel (1946)", trans. Bert Cardullo, first published in Esprit (1946), reprinted in Qu'est-ce que le cinéma?, vol. 1, Ontologie et langage (Paris: Éditions du Cerf, 1958), 31–36.

⏵ State Funeral

(2019), dir. Sergei Loznitsa, 135 min, Netherlands / Lithuania

2:00:59 — 2:04:13 vkvideo.ru/video-109280427_456239428

2:10:06 — 2:12:00 vkvideo.ru/video-109280427_456239428 Same file, two fragments · 3 min 14 s + 1 min 54 s · the player has no out-point, stop by hand

Questions

  1. How many different places did you count? What connects these shots to one another apart from the cut?
  2. How do we know that all these people are weeping over the same thing — and at the same hour?
  3. Who could have seen this in 1953?
  4. Nobody here asserts anything out loud. You understood what you were being told all the same. How exactly?

What is authorial here and what is inherited. The scene of worldwide mourning was already the intended culminating montage of The Great Farewell back in 1953. Loznitsa barely touches it. What you are watching is the work of a Soviet editor, shown in full.

All of Loznitsa's authorial editing in this passage comes down to a single juncture — between this scene and the closing titles. One cut turns two hours around.

Removing the voice-over does not mean ceasing to construct: the persuasion simply moves into the junctures, where it is harder to contest, because a cut cannot be quoted the way a line of narration can. And the reverse holds too: one cut is enough to turn around a two-hour construction.

The unit of meaning is the juncture, not the shot.

3 — The cut as limit

Part one · 13 min · post-utopian montage

Lead-in before the screening: after the Holocaust, montage stops promising a future. It begins to describe the impossibility of telling.

⏵ Night and Fog [Nuit et brouillard]

(1956), dir. Alain Resnais, 32 min, France

06:07 — 11:45 ok.ru/video/4422944950921 This player takes no time parameter — scrub to 06:07 by hand · 5 min 38 s

Questions

  1. What does the music do — does it help you feel, or does it get in the way?
  2. Why the different colours?
  3. What did you learn from the image, and what from the voice?
  4. The camera travels across an empty place. Who is travelling? Whose movement is this?
  5. What does this fragment make of you — someone who has been given an explanation, or someone who has been placed before a question?

Post-utopian montage: a critique of ready-made historical explanation. The rupture shows the working of trauma instead of "overcoming" it. The point is not that it must not be depicted, but that realist representation would make the event consumable.

Trauma here is not a theme but a form: repetition, rupture, deferral, the impossibility of integration. Montage does not depict trauma; it has its contours.

4 — The cut as disjunction

Part one · 15 min · historicizing and multiplying montage

⏵ Respite [Aufschub]

(2007), dir. Harun Farocki, 40 min, silent, b&w, Germany — reworking footage commissioned by the Westerbork camp commandant in 1944 and shot by Rudolf Breslauer

32:16 — 36:30 youtu.be/ih-5_-CL_T4 Player starts and stops on the marks · 4 min 14 s · silent

Questions

  1. What did we see, and what do we know only from our own historical knowledge?
  2. People in front of a camera behave differently than without it. Does that make the shot less truthful — or is that precisely its principal testimony?
  3. What do the stops, repetitions and titles do with the footage?

Soft montage and the dialectical cut. An image should not only be used but investigated. Two images do not destroy each other and are not reduced to a common conclusion; they remain side by side and hold the tension. It makes very familiar footage strange again.

Interstitial thinking. The strength of Respite lies not in seamlessness but in disjunctures. The narrator speaks in the first person and constantly relativises his own reading — he does not possess the images any more than the viewer does.

Laura Rascaroli, How the Essay Film Thinks (New York: Oxford University Press, 2017), ch. 2, "Montage: Essayistic Thinking at the Juncture of Images".

What kind of world are we editing in now?

Part one · multiplying montage

  1. The world is no longer broken into fragments. It is made of them.
  2. Kukulin calls this multiplying montage. It cuts between kinds of material, not between shots. Newsreel, a document, a screenshot, phone footage, a meme, a title card. Not different pictures — different kinds of thing in one line.
  3. The old montage assembled the world. This one assembles a person.
  4. A reel is a multiplying form by definition. You already work this way. The question is whether it is reflected upon or not.

So what does it mean to be critical inside this mode? You cannot step out of the feed. You can make the gaps do work. Every juncture has to produce something.

Test

Remove any fragment. If nothing changes, it is a feed, not a montage.

Ilya Kukulin, Mashiny zashumevshego vremeni: kak sovetskii montazh stal metodom neofitsial'noi kul'tury [Machines of the Noisy Time: How Soviet Montage Became a Method of Unofficial Culture] (Moscow: Novoe literaturnoe obozrenie, 2015) — untranslated; cite the Russian.

Assignment

Part two · 8 min · one assignment for all four groups

Assemble a sixty-second reel advertising an exhibition about the capture of Berlin at the Karlshorst museum.

Material — pre-cut shots from

⏵ Berlin

(1945), dir. Yuli Raizman — best documentary, Cannes 1946

⏵ The True Glory

(1945), dir. Garson Kanin and Carol Reed — Academy Award

One Soviet film, one British-American. The folder of pre-cut shots is handed out at the start of part two.

The condition in which the whole conflict of the first hour is lodged:

The reel must work as advertising — and at the same time not lie about the material.

Advertising by definition wants to stitch things together and to convince, which makes it constructive by definition. The task is to hold both requirements at once. How exactly, each group decides for itself.

What each group brings out of the breakout

  • a sequence of exactly 60 seconds
  • an explanation of the choice of shots and of that structure

Editing decision checklist

Before fixing a sequence, answer in writing

1 · Question
What question organises the work?
2 · This fragment
Why this particular fragment? What does it do that no other element does?
3 · The juncture
What connection does this juncture create — causality, contrast, analogy, rhyme, repetition, doubt, rupture?
4 · Found or made
Did this connection exist in the material, or am I creating it? Can I justify it, and do I indicate the degree of my own certainty?
5 · What disappears
What does my structure make invisible? Which voice, context or source disappears when I cut things down?
6 · The viewer
What position does the viewer occupy? Do they receive an explanation, investigate alongside me, experience, judge — or come up against the impossibility of a conclusion?
Carry-over from module 03
  1. Module 03 asked what you did to a single shot and whether you could write it down. This one asks what you did between two shots — and a juncture leaves no metadata.
  2. If a caption over one frame is a claim, what is a cut between two?
  3. Which of the six answers above would you actually publish next to the reel?
