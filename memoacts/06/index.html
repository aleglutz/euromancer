Before we start

18:05 · 5 min · checked out loud, once

Three files, on your own machine, not in a browser tab. If one of them is missing, say so now — a tutor will get you to a workable state during the demonstration, not during the practice block.

What you need open

  • Script — uploaded to the shared Google Drive folder
  • Are.na board — at least 10 images or clips, downloaded to your device
  • Music — one track from a free library, downloaded. Voluntary
  • Editor — installed, opens your files, exports 1080×1920 at 30 fps

Downloaded means downloaded. An Are.na board that lives online is a reference, not material — the practice block has no time in it for fetching images one at a time.

Where the music comes from

Free libraries: Pixabay Music, Free Music Archive, Ende. Choose something that fits the mood of your piece rather than something you like on its own. Note the track name and the licence as you download — the credits will need both.

Carried over from Module 05

Built-in assets music, sounds, fonts and stickers bundled inside an editor are licensed for use inside that app. Keep them out of the final mix — the reel is published under the museum's name and the funder's
Tonight the demonstration is in CapCut because it is the fastest thing to watch. Every move shown has an equivalent in Shotcut, Resolve and Kdenlive, and the techniques are what you are being asked to learn — not the buttons
Sound take it from a CC0 source and write down where it came from

Demonstration

18:10 · 40 min · built from scratch, questions taken as they come

An empty project to an exported file, in order, with nothing skipped. Interrupt when something happens too fast — this is the block where that is free.

What gets shown, in this order

StepWhat is actually being decided
Audio and subtitles the read lands first and everything else is measured against it. Subtitles are typed from the script you already have — not recognised from the audio, so no name, rank or date can be misspelt
Photos and footage which image stands with which sentence. This is the one decision the software cannot make for you
Motion and animation slow zoom, pan, push. How much movement an image's pixels can carry before it softens — an archival scan has a ceiling and the ceiling is visible
Effects glitch, grain, light leaks, transitions. Which of them carry a claim about the material and which are decoration
Sound design ambience under the voice, accents on cuts, the music bed and where it ducks. Where the sound does the persuading instead of the image
Details that raise quality cutting on the sentence and not on the beat, leaving silence, safe margins for captions on a vertical frame, consistent headroom, the export preset

Watch what is being decided, not which menu it lives in. You will be doing this in your own editor thirty minutes from now, and the menu will be somewhere else.

Practice — 30 minutes

18:50 · 30 min · your own editor · tutors on call

Fully edit the first fifteen seconds of your video, from your script, with images from your board. More than fifteen seconds if the time allows. Not polished — legible. The point is to show you can do each of the six things that were just demonstrated.

Space starts and pauses · R resets · The clock is shared, the phases are a guide

Ask for a tutor at any point. Say what you are stuck on rather than sharing your screen and waiting — the block is thirty minutes for everyone at once.

Your fifteen seconds must contain

  • Subtitles
  • A background and visual elements — images and/or video
  • Motion or animation on the images
  • Effects, and filters if you want them
  • Sound design
  • Background music · voluntary
  • A finished export

If you finish early, extend past fifteen seconds rather than polishing what you have. Length now is worth more to you than finish — the polish pass is what the week between this module and the final screening is for.

Screening and feedback

19:30 · 20 min · around five videos, voluntary

Per video

  • it plays — no introduction from the author
  • the author says what they were trying to do
  • response — one tutor leads

What the response stays on

  • Whether the images are placed against the lines they belong to, or merely in sequence.
  • Whether the movement was chosen for the image or applied to all of them at once.
  • Where the sound is doing work the picture is not, and whether that was decided.
  • Which effect would be missed if it were removed.

Fifteen seconds is a short thing to have made and a fair thing to be judged on, because everyone made theirs under the same clock. Nothing said here is about the finished film — it is about what to do in the next five days.

Homework

Deadline: 18:00 CEST, 7 September

1 — Compile your content on Hotglue

Create a page on hotglue.me and upload your materials there: a storyboard, or the full video based on your script if you managed to edit it.

Attach a brief document alongside the file. For every piece of media used — image, video, audio — specify its source.

The session between this module and the final screening takes place on 3–6 September and lasts sixty minutes. Come with any issue you were unable to resolve this evening. It is the last session with a tutor before the screening on 7 September.

The criteria you are assessed against were set out in Module 05 and have not changed.
